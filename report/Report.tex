\section{Introduction}
In the context of the Data Challenge proposed by Idemia, the objective is to predict the percentage of facial occlusion in "in the wild" images (224x224 pixels). The major challenge lies in handling the training data imbalance (a low proportion of highly occluded faces) and the strict gender fairness requirement imposed by the evaluation metric.

In this report, we present our solution based on multi-task learning, designed to extract robust features while minimizing the demographic bias penalty.

\section{Exploratory Data Analysis}

The exploratory data analysis performed on the train database revealed that only a low proportion of the faces show a high occlusion
value $\approx 0.4$ (see Fig.\ref{fig:occlusion_distribution}) and an unbalance distribution in terms of gender, i.e. Men = 67.6 \% and Women = 32.4 \%
(see Fig.\ref{fig:gender_distribution}).

From Fig.\ref{fig:occlusion_distribution} we can observe a right-skewed distribution. We can observe a longer tail on the right side, which
indicates that the concentration is more towards the lower values and the extreme high values (outliers) drag the tail towards the right side.

In order to better understand the visual impact of the occlusion levels, random samples of different occlusion intervals were inspected (see Fig.\ref{fig:occlusion_mosaic}).
