\section{Introduction}
In the context of the Data Challenge proposed by Idemia, the objective is to predict the percentage of facial occlusion
in images (224x224 pixels). The major challenge lies in handling the training data imbalance (a low proportion of the training database
contain highly occluded faces) and the gender fairness requirement imposed by the evaluation metric.

In this report, we present our solution based on multi-task learning and data augmentaion, designed to extract robust features
while minimizing the demographic bias penalty.

\section{Exploratory Data Analysis}
\label{sec:eda}

The exploratory data analysis (EDA) performed on the train database revealed that only a low proportion of the faces show a high occlusion.
This is verified by Fig.\ref{fig:train_occlusion_distribution}, where we can observe a right-skewed distribution: the samples are more concentrated
on the lower values than in the extreme high values, whici drag the tail towards the right side.

From a gender perspective, it was found that the train database contains 67.6 \% of men and 32.4 \% of women (see Fig.\ref{fig:gender_distribution}).

In order to understand what different occlusion levels looked-like, images were sampled out from the train database
using different occlusion intervals, \textit{i.e.} $Low \in [0.1, 0.3]$, $Medium \in [0.31, 0.4]$ and $High \in [0.7, 1.0]$.
These intervals were defined based on the test database occlusion distribution provided with the Data Challenge (see Fig.\ref{fig:test_occlusion_distribution}).
The results of this sampling process allow to make some interesting observations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $low$ occluded images trend to provide a global visibility of the face forehead, eyes, nose, chicks and chin.
    It's worth noting that the neck is not visible. 
    \item in $medium$ occluded images par of the faces are covered either with the hair, a hat, hand, fingers and even physical objects.
    \item  Finally in $high$ occluded images faces seem to be mostly visible, however a blurry / noised effect was noticed.
    It's quite interesting that there were only 5 images in this interval. These can defnitely be considered as outliers.
\end{itemize}

Figure \ref{fig:occlusion_mosaic} provides simples for each interval. The above-presented findings will be tackled during in section \ref{sec:data-augmentation}.

\subsection{Data augmentation}
\label{sec:data-augmentation}

The goal of this section is to increase the diversity of the train database in order to match close enough
the distribution of the test database. The results from the EDA in section \ref{sec:eda} showed that 
in $medium$ occluded faces part of their faces are covered by some "object", \textit{i.e.} hair, hand, hat, etc.
On the other hand, $high$ occluded images are mainly blurry / noised. In order to consider for these variations,
to data augmentation strategies were considered: 1) kernel filtering and 2) random erasing.

%Afin d'atténuer le \textit{Domain Shift}, nous avons utilisé \texttt{RandomErasing} pour générer artificiellement des masques noirs aléatoires sur le set d'entraînement, forçant le modèle à traiter des cas d'occlusion extrêmes.
